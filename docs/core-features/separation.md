# Separation Processes and Well Stream Factors

Describes the separation process used to isolate natural gas from water, sediment, and natural gas liquids in raw extracted streams. Explains well stream factors and how they account for measurement impurities to ensure accurate gas volume calculations.

## Separation at the Well Site

Raw hydrocarbon streams extracted from production wells contain a mixture of natural gas, water, sediment, rocks, dirt, and natural gas liquids. At the well site or shortly after, a separator is used to begin the separation process. The separator uses gravity and physical properties to separate the components: water and sediment settle to the bottom, while natural gas rises to the top. However, not all well sites have separators, particularly at the initial production point.

- Separators are used to separate mixed hydrocarbon streams into components.
- Water and sediment separate by gravity to the bottom of the separator.
- Natural gas, being lighter, rises to the top.
- Not all well sites have separators installed.
- Separators may be permanently installed or mobile (test separators).

## Well Stream Factors and Measurement Accuracy

When meters are installed in unseparated or partially separated streams, they measure everything present (gas, liquids, solids) rather than just pure natural gas. This causes over-measurement. Well stream factors account for this inaccuracy by calculating what portion of the measured volume is actually pure natural gas versus impurities. For example, a meter might measure 1000 cubic feet of fluid, but after accounting for the well stream factor, only 980 cubic feet is determined to be pure natural gas. This factor is calculated using test separators and provided to FLOWCAL for accurate flow calculations.

- Well stream factors correct for over-measurement in unseparated streams.
- Test separators are used to calculate well stream factors.
- Factors account for the presence of water, liquids, and solids.
- A measured volume of 1000 cubic feet might actually represent 980 cubic feet of pure gas.
- FLOWCAL uses well stream factors in flow calculations and royalty determinations.

## Multi-Stage Separation

Separation continues at multiple points in the hydrocarbon production process. Before gas enters major pipelines, comprehensive separation is performed to remove water, solids, and natural gas liquids. Natural gas liquids (NGLs) are valuable products that must be separated because they are worth more than methane alone. Multiple separators at different stages ensure progressive purification of the gas stream before pipeline entry.

- Separation occurs at multiple points in the production process.
- Progressive separation increases gas purity at each stage.
- Water, solids, and natural gas liquids are removed before pipeline entry.
- Natural gas liquids have higher value and are separated for independent sale.
- Quality specifications must be met before pipeline acceptance.
