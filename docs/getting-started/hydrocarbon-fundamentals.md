# Hydrocarbon Fundamentals

Introduction to hydrocarbons and the three primary fluid types measured in the energy industry: crude oil, natural gas, and water. Explains how these fluids are extracted from underground formations in mixed states and separated at various stages.

## What Are Hydrocarbons

Hydrocarbons are compounds made up of hydrogen and carbon elements. In the energy industry, three primary types of fluids are extracted and measured: crude oil (heavier hydrocarbon products), natural gas (lighter hydrocarbon mixture), and water (co-produced with hydrocarbons). These fluids are initially extracted as a mixture from underground formations and must be separated at various stages to be processed and transported.

- Hydrocarbons are hydrogen and carbon compounds extracted from underground formations.
- Three primary fluids in energy industry: crude oil, natural gas, and water.
- These fluids are initially extracted in mixed form.
- Separation processes are used at various stages to separate different hydrocarbon types.

## Natural Gas as a Hydrocarbon Mixture

Natural gas is not a pure single compound but rather a mixture of different hydrocarbon compounds. Methane is the largest component of natural gas, but it can also include ethane, propane, butane, and other compounds. The exact composition varies depending on the geological formation from which it is extracted.

- Natural gas is a mixture of hydrocarbon compounds, not a single pure substance.
- Methane is the largest component of natural gas.
- Natural gas can contain ethane, propane, butane, and other compounds.
- Composition varies by geological source.

## Mixed Extraction from Underground

When hydrocarbons are initially extracted from underground formations, they come up mixed with other substances including water, sediment, rocks, dirt, hydrogen sulfide, and natural gas liquids. This mixed stream must be separated to isolate and quantify each product type for measurement, quality control, and regulatory purposes.

- Extracted hydrocarbon streams are initially mixed with water, sediment, and other materials.
- Hydrogen sulfide and other compounds may be present in raw extractions.
- Natural gas liquids are valuable co-products that must be separated.
- Separation and purification are necessary for accurate measurement and quality control.
