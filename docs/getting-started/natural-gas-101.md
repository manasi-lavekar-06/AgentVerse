# Natural Gas Measurement Process Overview

Comprehensive overview of natural gas measurement through the entire production and distribution chain from extraction to end user. Shows how measurement occurs at critical points including production wells, separators, gathering systems, pipelines, and distribution systems, and where FLOWCAL fits in managing this measurement data.

## The Complete Natural Gas Flow Process

The natural gas measurement process spans the entire value chain from underground extraction to delivery to end users. It begins at production wells where natural gas is extracted from underground formations. Gas then moves through separation processes to remove water and other compounds, into gathering systems that consolidate multiple wells, through transmission pipelines to distribution systems, and finally to end users. Measurement occurs at multiple points throughout this chain wherever there is a change in ownership or regulatory requirement. FLOWCAL's role is to receive data from these measurement points, perform calculations, and provide reports.

- Measurement spans production, processing, transmission, and distribution.
- Natural gas flows from wells to consumers through multiple stages.
- Measurement occurs at ownership transfer and regulatory points.
- FLOWCAL integrates measurement data from multiple systems.
- FLOWCAL calculations provide accurate volume and royalty reporting.

## Measurement Points in the Process

Critical measurement points in natural gas flow include: the well head (though often with inaccurate data requiring well stream factors); after separation processing; at gathering system entry points; at major pipeline intersections and custody transfer points; at compressor stations (for fuel consumption); at pipeline inlet and outlets; at distribution city gates; and at end-user delivery points. Each measurement point serves specific accounting or regulatory purposes. Where ownership changes, measurement ensures accurate billing and royalty payments. Where regulatory requirements exist, measurement provides compliance documentation.

- Well head: initial measurement (often with well stream factors).
- After separation: cleaner gas streams for more accurate measurement.
- Gathering system: consolidation points for multiple wells.
- Custody transfer points: ownership change accounting.
- Compressor stations: fuel consumption tracking.
- Distribution city gates: wholesale-to-retail transition.
- End-user meters: billing and delivery verification.

## FLOWCAL's Role in the Measurement Process

FLOWCAL is not a measurement device but rather a data management and calculation system. It receives measurement data from meters installed at various points in the natural gas system. Using this data, FLOWCAL performs calculations to determine actual volume flow, applies corrections like well stream factors, tracks line pack volumes, calculates compressor fuel consumption, and produces reports for billing, royalty accounting, and regulatory compliance. FLOWCAL integrates with TestIt and other Quorum solutions to provide a complete measurement and data management ecosystem.

- FLOWCAL receives data from multiple meter sources.
- FLOWCAL performs calculations on raw measurement data.
- FLOWCAL applies well stream factors for measurement accuracy.
- FLOWCAL manages line pack volume tracking.
- FLOWCAL supports calculated meters for cost-effective measurement.
- FLOWCAL produces reports for accounting and compliance.
- FLOWCAL integrates with TestIt for data quality assurance.
