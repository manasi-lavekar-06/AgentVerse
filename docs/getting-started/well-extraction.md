# Well Extraction and Hydraulic Fracturing

Explains the two primary methods for extracting natural gas from underground: conventional drilling and hydraulic fracturing. Details how hydraulic fracturing uses water and sand at high pressure to fracture tight rock formations and release trapped gas.

## Production Wells and Extraction Methods

A production well is an area where natural gas is extracted from the ground. There are two primary extraction methods: conventional wells where operators drill straight down through the formation, and fracking (hydraulic fracturing) operations used when conventional methods are insufficient. The extraction method depends on the geological characteristics of the formation and how the gas is held in the rock.

- Production wells are the starting point for hydrocarbon extraction.
- Conventional drilling: drilling straight down through the formation.
- Hydraulic fracturing (fracking): used for tight or resistant formations.
- Choice of method depends on geological formation characteristics.

## Hydraulic Fracturing (Fracking) Process

Hydraulic fracturing is a technique used to extract gas from tight rock formations where conventional drilling is ineffective. The process involves pumping water and sand (called proppant) along with chemicals down into the well at very high pressure. The water fractures the rock by force, and the sand props open the fractures in the rock. This creates pathways for the gas to move out of the rock into the wellbore pipe, where it can be produced. The term 'hydraulic fracturing' comes from the use of water (hydraulic) to fracture the rock.

- Hydraulic fracturing extracts gas from tight rock formations.
- Process pumps water, sand (proppant), and chemicals down the well.
- Water is pumped at very high pressure to fracture the rock.
- Sand props open the fractures, creating gas flow pathways.
- Gas moves from rock formation into the wellbore for production.
- Alternative to conventional drilling for resistant formations.

## Drilling Completion and Production

Whether using conventional or hydraulic fracturing methods, a pipe is installed in the well to capture the extracted gas. In some conventional approaches, explosives may be used to shoot holes in both the pipe and rock to allow gas flow. Once fractures or holes are established, gas flows through these pathways into the pipe and is produced at the surface for processing.

- Pipe is installed in the wellbore to capture extracted gas.
- Conventional methods may use explosives to create flow paths.
- Fractures provide pathways for gas to reach the wellbore.
- Gas flows through established pathways into the collection pipe.
